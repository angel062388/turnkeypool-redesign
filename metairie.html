How to Conduct Background Research on Potential Contractors in Metairie

  • 01

    Online reviews and ratings

    Websites such as Yelp, Google, and Angie's List provide platforms for customers to share their experiences and opinions about various contractors. By reading these reviews, you can gain insights into the quality of their work, their professionalism, and their reliability. Online ratings can give you a quick overview of their overall reputation and level of customer satisfaction as well. It is important to consider the overall trend of reviews and ratings rather than solely relying on one or two individual opinions.

  • 02

    Consultation meetings

    It is important to schedule meetings with the contractors you are considering to hire for your project. During these meetings, you can ask them about their experience, qualifications, and past projects. You can also inquire about their licensing and insurance coverage. It is crucial to discuss your specific requirements and expectations for the project to ensure that the contractor is capable of meeting them. Moreover, consulting with previous clients or reading reviews and testimonials can provide valuable insights into the contractor's reputation and the quality of their work.

  • 03

    Requesting quotes and proposals

    Firstly, it is important to gather a list of potential contractors by reaching out to local trade associations, asking for recommendations from friends or neighbors, and searching online directories. Once you have a list of potential contractors, it is crucial to conduct thorough research on each one. This can involve checking their license and insurance status, verifying their experience and track record by looking at their portfolio or previous clients, and checking for any complaints or legal issues by reviewing online reviews, forums, or contacting the Better Business Bureau. In Addition, it is advisable to request quotes and proposals from each contractor and compare them based on factors such as cost, timeline, and scope of work.

06 · The Area

All About Metairie, LA

About Metairie

Metairie is a bustling city located in Jefferson Parish, Louisiana, and is part of the greater New Orleans metropolitan area. As of 2020, it boasted a population of 143,507, making it the largest community within Jefferson Parish and ranking as the fifth-largest CDP in the United States at the time. Interestingly, if Metairie were incorporated as a city, it would be Louisiana's fourth-largest, surpassing Shreveport.

Situated in eastern Jefferson Parish, Metairie borders several key locations. To the east lies New Orleans, with Kenner to the west. North of Metairie you'll find Lake Pontchartrain, a stunning freshwater lake, while the Illinois Central Railroad tracks mark the southern border.

Things To Do

Shop till you drop at Lakeside Shopping Center: The largest mall in the New Orleans area, Lakeside Shopping Center boasts over 240 stores and restaurants, making it a shopper's paradise. From national department stores and popular apparel brands to specialty shops and local boutiques, there's something for everyone at Lakeside.

Cheer on the New Orleans Saints at Caesars Superdome: Experience the electrifying atmosphere of a New Orleans Saints game at Caesars Superdome, a multi-purpose dome stadium that's home to the NFL's New Orleans Saints and the Sugar Bowl.

Explore the Metairie Cemetery: Step back in time with a visit to Metairie Cemetery, one of the largest cemeteries in the United States. Founded in 1872, this sprawling cemetery is a fascinating place to wander, with its ornate tombs, statues, and mausoleums reflecting the city's rich history and cultural heritage.

Get active on the Lafitte Greenway: The Lafitte Greenway is a 2.6-mile paved trail that connects Metairie to New Orleans. Perfect for walking, running, biking, or rollerblading, the Greenway offers a scenic route to explore the area and get some exercise.

Notable Residents

Patrick Eugene Carr: was a United States district judge of the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Louisiana.

James Andrew Crowell IV: an associate judge of the Superior Court of the District of Columbia.

Wendy Jane Dascomb: an American model and beauty pageant titleholder who was crowned Miss USA 1969.

Ellen Lee DeGeneres: an American comedian, television host, actress, and writer.

Nearby Suburbs

Kenner: Bordering Metairie to the west, Kenner is known for its family-friendly atmosphere and exciting attractions. One such attraction is the expansive Kenner City Park, featuring a sprawling lake, a public golf course, and a fun amusement park perfect for a day of family fun. History buffs will appreciate a visit to the Pontchartrain Center, a former shopping mall that's now a museum chronicling the history of Kenner and the surrounding area.

River Ridge: Head north from Metairie and you'll find yourself in River Ridge, a peaceful suburban community with a strong sense of community. Nature enthusiasts will enjoy spending time at the River Ridge Village Park, which offers walking trails, a playground, and a fishing pier along the scenic shores of Lake Pontchartrain. If you're looking for a delicious meal, River Ridge boasts a vibrant restaurant scene serving everything from casual fare to fine dining.

Harahan: Situated directly south of Metairie, Harahan is a quaint town with a rich history. Take a step back in time with a stroll through the Harahan City Park, a charming green space featuring a historic carousel and a peaceful rose garden. Art lovers will appreciate the Weinzimmer Art Museum, which houses a collection of contemporary and regional art.

Elmwood: Nestled southwest of Metairie, Elmwood offers a relaxed suburban atmosphere with a touch of southern charm. Foodies will be delighted by the diverse culinary scene in Elmwood, with options ranging from authentic Cajun cuisine to international flavors. Outdoor enthusiasts can explore the Woodland Playground, which provides a playground, picnic areas, and a walking trail.

Interesting / Fun Facts

French Connection: The name "Metairie" comes from the French term "métairie," which refers to a system of sharecropping where tenants pay rent as a portion of their crops. This hints at Metairie's agricultural beginnings when French colonists first settled the area in the 1760s.

Technically Not a City: Despite its large population, Metairie is actually a census-designated place (CDP) and not an incorporated city. This means it lacks some of the self-governance that comes with city status. As of 2020, if Metairie were incorporated, it would be Louisiana's fourth-largest city!

Shopping Paradise: Metairie boasts the title of having the highest-grossing mall in the New Orleans metropolitan area – the Lakeside Shopping Center. This massive mall attracts many visitors due to its convenient location near the airport and its tax-free shopping opportunities.

Final Resting Place for Notable Figures: Metairie Cemetery is one of the largest in the United States. While not exactly residents, some interesting people are buried here, including Stan Rice (poet, artist, and husband of Anne Rice), Ève Curie (daughter of Nobel Prize winner Marie Curie and a renowned journalist), and Andrew Higgins (inventor of the PT boats used in World War II).

Public Transportation

RTA Buses: The New Orleans Regional Transit Authority (RTA) operates several bus routes that service Metairie.

Ride-Sharing Services: Ride-sharing services like Uber and Lyft are readily available in Metairie and can be a convenient option for getting around, especially for first-time visitors unfamiliar with the bus routes.

Taxi: While less common than in major cities, you might be able to hail a taxi on the street, particularly near popular areas like shopping centers or hotels.
